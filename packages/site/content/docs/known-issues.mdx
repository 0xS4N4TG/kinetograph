---
title: Known issues
description: What doesn't work yet in Kinetograph, and what you see instead.
---

Each item says what doesn't work and what you see instead. For what does work, see [Remotion compatibility](/docs/compat/). Items marked "reported" show up in the warnings and in `doctor`.

## Not planned

- **`IFrame`.** Not drawn, because a real webpage needs a browser. It is reported as unsupported content.
- **Not exported.** `Internals`, `FolderContext`, `PathInternals` and `AudioForPreview` are internal to Remotion.
- **Replaced by Kinetograph's own tools.** `@remotion/three` and `@remotion/player` are replaced by the engine's own 3D layer and studio. `renderer`, `bundler`, `cli` and `lambda` are replaced by the Kinetograph CLI.
- **Tailwind packages.** `tailwind` and `tailwind-v4` are webpack configuration only, so they do nothing here. Tailwind itself works.

## Partly working

### Effects and `HtmlInCanvas`

- `2d`, `webgl2` and `webgpu` effects (`createEffect`) run, and so do the `onPaint` and `onInit` props, with `getContext('webgpu')` in them. A WebGPU effect gets a recording `gpuDevice`; the picture before it is a source for `queue.copyExternalImageToTexture` (an upload from the layer, so the destination is `rgba8unorm` or `rgba8unorm-srgb`).
- A `webgpu` effect, `onInit` or `onPaint` that makes a call listed under WebGPU below as not drawn is drawn without the effect (or without `onPaint`) and reported.
- State an effect keeps from `setup` does not carry to the next frame. Each frame runs `setup`, `apply` and `cleanup` again.
- `getImageData` of the source is not supported.
- An `async` `apply`, `onInit` or `onPaint` holds the frame until its promise settles (as `delayRender` does), so what it draws after an `await` is in the frame, the same alone as in a sequence. `onPaint` waits for `onInit`, and the cleanup `onInit` returns runs after `onPaint`. A rejection is reported for that frame and the picture is drawn without the stage. The stage runs once per frame; promises that wait on a timer need real time, not `setTimeout`, which the render's virtual clock does not advance.
- An `<HtmlInCanvas>` inside another is not supported.

### Video and audio encoding and decoding in your own code

`@remotion/webcodecs` is Remotion's wrapper around the browser's WebCodecs API, which encodes and decodes video and audio frames. Its encode and decode functions, and the box tree and sample callbacks of `@remotion/media-parser`, throw `UnsupportedApiError`. They need a video decoder and encoder in the addon, which is not built. Normal `<Video>` and `<Audio>` are not affected.

### HyperFrames projects

- **Three.js.** Not drawn. A page that loads Three.js is reported on every frame.
- **WebGPU.** Shader blocks that use `shaders.iife.js` (`aurora`, `blob`, `ripples`, `swirl` and the like) are drawn natively. See [WebGPU](#webgpu).
- **Import maps and ES modules that import.** Not run. They are reported.
- **Selectors that reach into an `<svg>` from outside it.** The engine applies the page's CSS cascade to an `<svg>`, including rules that match through an ancestor. Two things it does not reach, so the loader still writes those rules onto the shapes as inline style once the scripts have run: shapes that a script builds after a fetch, and `transform`, `clip-path`, `mask` and `filter` (the cascade never reads them). Rules inside `@media` are written when the media query holds for the composition's size. A class added to a shape later, by a tween or a script, is not restyled.
- **Timing.** `data-start` references stay inside one composition. Audio effects (`data-fx-chain`) and `fx.*` automation lanes are ignored. A `rate` lane on audio is played in quarter-second slices at constant rates.
- **Remote pictures set by a script.** `fetch-deps` finds the `https` pictures in the markup and the CSS (`<img>`, `srcset`, `poster`, SVG `<image>`, `url()`), not one a script assigns at run time. Such a picture is reported on every frame and not drawn; put it in the markup or the CSS.
- **Colour grading.** `lut`, `hueCurves`, `secondaries`, `details` (vignette, grain) and `effects` are not drawn (the engine has `lut` and `grain` effects, but a DOM node cannot take an effect; `feImage` is not supported, so a vignette cannot be a filter). `shadows`, `highlights`, `wheels` and `vibrance` are approximated.
- **CSS.** `html` and `body` rules apply to two wrapper elements.
- **`white-space: nowrap` around inline-blocks.** In an absolutely positioned box without a width, inline-block children that do not fit wrap to a new line instead of staying on one (`mk-clone-wall-transition`). The layout is the engine's.
- **Canvas 2D gradients** interpolate in premultiplied colour, as CSS gradients do. A canvas gradient from `rgba(0,0,0,0)` to an opaque colour, drawn by a browser in straight alpha, passes through dark shades; here it does not (`transitions-destruction`).
- **WebGL depth test.** Instanced drawing works, but `code-slice-hero` draws its instanced quads with the depth test on, and a draw with `DEPTH_TEST` enabled is not supported (see [WebGL](#webgl)). Its seek fails at frame 24 of `doctor`'s 240 with `unsupported: drawing with the depth test on`, and the failure is reported on every frame.
- **`offset-path`** motion is not drawn.
- **Variable-font advance widths.** Text in a variable font that sets `font-variation-settings` per element measures about 13% wider than in Chrome for `Recursive` (`weight-wave`), so a line fitted by `measureText` wraps.
- **Slow load.** Scripts that read many computed styles run slowly because the engine restyles the whole island for every element asked about; `claude-exchange` takes about 20 s before frame 0.

### Canvas 2D

- A pattern of a canvas, used for a stroke or for text, is drawn transparent.
- Pattern fills work, up to 10,000 tiles.
- `isPointInStroke` ignores caps, joins and dashes.
- `url()` filters are reported and not drawn.
- Shadow and filter images are capped at 50 million pixels per canvas layer per frame. A frame past that is an error.

### WebGL

Every frame starts with an empty canvas and blank framebuffer textures, also for a context made with `preserveDrawingBuffer`, because a frame is a function of its own number. A frame that draws nothing draws nothing, where a browser would keep the last picture. Each item below needs new GPU work.

- Not supported: renderbuffers; depth and stencil attachments; `COLOR_ATTACHMENT1` and up; a texture level above 0 as a target; targets that are not RGBA or that hold uploaded pixels; `generateMipmap` on a texture drawn into; `LINES`, `LINE_STRIP` and `LINE_LOOP`; instanced points; a `vertexAttribDivisor` above 1; cube, 3D and float textures; and multisampled edges.
- `readPixels` is refused by design.
- Points are drawn as squares of `gl_PointSize` pixels with `gl_PointCoord`, at most 511 pixels across (`ALIASED_POINT_SIZE_RANGE` is 1 to 511). `gl_VertexID` in a vertex shader that draws points is the point's index, but with `drawElements` it is the position in the index list, not the index.
- `blitFramebuffer` copies the colour only, as there is no depth or stencil buffer, and refuses to read and write one buffer.
- `texSubImage2D` into a texture a framebuffer drew in is supported from a canvas, `ImageData` or an array of pixels; from an image file it is not.

### WebGPU

A canvas's `getContext('webgpu')` records, and the passes it submits are drawn on the engine's own device. Drawn: buffers (uniform, storage, vertex, index), WGSL modules, bind group layouts, render pipelines (blend, topology, culling, vertex buffers, `layout: 'auto'`), bind groups, render passes into the canvas or into a 2D texture, `draw`, `drawIndexed`, viewport, scissor, `writeBuffer`, `copyBufferToBuffer`, and textures and samplers:

- 2D textures of `rgba8unorm`, `rgba8unorm-srgb`, `bgra8unorm`, `bgra8unorm-srgb`, `r8unorm`, `rg8unorm` and `rgba16float`, with mip levels; `createView` (levels), `createSampler` (address modes, filters, lod clamps, anisotropy); texture and sampler bind group entries, in explicit layouts and in `layout: 'auto'`.
- `queue.writeTexture`, `copyBufferToTexture`, `copyTextureToTexture`, and `queue.copyExternalImageToTexture` from `ImageData`, `<img>`, `ImageBitmap` (`createImageBitmap` of an image, a canvas, `ImageData` or a `Blob` of an image file), a 2D `<canvas>` or `OffscreenCanvas`, a `<video>` and a layer (in an effect). A video gives the frame at `video.currentTime` (set it before the copy; the frame is the one a video layer shows at that time, so it is the same alone and in a sequence). `flipY` and `premultipliedAlpha` are honoured, and `copyExternalImageToTexture` goes into the four 8-bit RGBA formats only.
- Render passes into a texture, so multi-pass shaders work. A texture is the steps that wrote it, in order; the steps of an earlier frame stay for later frames until a whole write (a `clear` pass, a whole `writeTexture` or copy) covers them, so a page that redraws a texture each frame is the same alone and in a sequence. A texture drawn with `loadOp: 'load'` every frame piles up and is not: the frame then depends on the frames before it.
- Every size, level, origin, row stride and usage is checked as the spec does, on the page side (it throws) and again when the frame is drawn (a warning on stderr and a transparent canvas). A frame may hold 512 MiB of textures (all levels counted) and 4096 steps; identical bytes are kept once.

Not supported, and reported on every frame (`--strict` fails): compute pipelines and storage textures, query sets, render bundles, indirect draws, depth and stencil, multisampling, texture arrays, 1D and 3D textures, `viewFormats`, comparison samplers, `importExternalTexture` (copy the frame with `copyExternalImageToTexture`), a video that is not a file of the project, reading a buffer or a texture back (`mapAsync`, `copyTextureToBuffer`), the canvas's own texture as a source or in a bind group, and other texture formats (`rgba32float`, depth, compressed).

- A `<video>` is read at `currentTime`, which a page sets itself; the engine does not play it. A video file that names no colour space is converted as a video layer is, which differs from Chrome's colour management for unlabelled video.
- The canvas is drawn as 8-bit `rgba8unorm`; `rgba16float` and a display-p3 `colorSpace` are reported and drawn in sRGB without extended range.
- A WGSL module naga refuses is reported and the canvas draws nothing; one that wgpu refuses later is a warning on stderr and a transparent canvas.
- The `shaders.iife.js` blocks of HyperFrames draw into one canvas without an input: none of them takes an image or a layer. The ones that read a texture they made themselves (`chrome-orb`, `frost`, `goo`, `heatmap`, `hologram`, `holographic`, `light-edge`, `liquid-metal`, `nebula`, `obsidian`, `plastic`, `thin-film`, `water`) also dispatch compute kernels that write `rgba32float` storage textures, so they are reported and drawn without the effect.

### WGSL `shader()`

It sees only its own layer, runs one pass, has no other texture, and takes `f32` uniforms only.

### 3D (`scene3d`)

- Not supported: occlusion and emissive texture maps, bevels and GPU skinning.
- The CPU deforms morphs and skins once per pose.
- At most 4 lights cast shadows, and 8 shadow maps are drawn in all. A point light takes 6 of them.

### CSS

- `background-repeat: space` with thousands of gradient tiles still errors. The other repeats are drawn as one tile repeated by `feTile`.
- Backdrop blur and glow are capped at 128 px. The `blur` effect takes up to 512.

### SVG

- A page rule reaches the shapes in an `<svg>` for fill, stroke, opacity, display, visibility, the font, `text-anchor`, `paint-order`, `shape-rendering` and `clip-rule`, but not for `transform`, `clip-path`, `mask` or `filter` on an element inside it: set those as attributes or inline style.
- A rule that sets a property to its initial value on a shape with a presentation attribute for it (`.bar { fill: black }` over `fill="red"`) does not win: the value looks like inheritance.

### GSAP

- GSAP's root clock follows the frame, so a `gsap.to` that plays by itself advances ([GSAP](/docs/compat/#gsap)). A jump to a later frame (a still, a worker starting at frame 600) renders the frames it skips, without reading them, so the effects that make tweens at an intermediate frame run there. That costs one React render per skipped frame in a project that imports `gsap`, also when it only seeks paused timelines.
- Motion-blur sub-frames do not move GSAP's clock: a tween that plays by itself is at the frame's own state in every sub-frame. A paused timeline you seek yourself is blurred as usual.
- `ScrollTrigger` and `Draggable` respond to scrolling and dragging, which a render does not have, so they do not apply. `Flip` works.
- An SVG shape's `getBoundingClientRect` is worked out from its `getBBox`, its transforms and the `<svg>`'s `viewBox`, not from layout. Strokes and filters are not in it.
- More in [GSAP](/docs/compat/#gsap).

### Libraries

- A rendered frame is only as frame-pure as the library: Motion's `animate()` that plays by itself is (see [Libraries](/docs/compat/#libraries-that-run-their-own-frame-loop)), but one that reads the wall clock or a real `Date` outside the virtual one is not.
- Motion picks the Web Animations API when `element.animate` exists. The engine's `animate()` throws, so Motion falls back to its own JavaScript animation, whose colour mixing differs slightly from the browser's.
- Chart.js, Recharts and the rest measure text only with the fonts you registered. With none, text is measured and drawn in the built-in font.

### `@remotion/skia` and `@shopify/react-native-skia`

- `Atlas`, `Patch`, `Glyphs`, `TextBlob`, `Picture` and `ImageSVG` are drawn. Text inside an SVG is not.
- Not supported: Reanimated values, `FitBox`, `Skia.Surface` and `Skia.Offscreen`, the `Skia.Image`, `Skia.Data` and `Skia.Typeface` factories, `useVideo`, animated images, paragraph measurement, and shaders or filters set on a `Skia.Paint()` object.
- Drawing is on the CPU only.
- PNGs of drawn frames are kept under `~/.cache/kinetograph/skia`, the newest 400 to 600 of them.
- More in [AUTHORING.md](https://github.com/0xS4N4TG/kinetograph/blob/main/packages/cli/skills/kinetograph/references/AUTHORING.md), section 7.7.

### `@remotion/rive`

- Not supported: `onLoad`, `assetLoader`, `effects` and the `ref` getters.
- A state machine starts with its inputs at their defaults, and you can't set them.
- Only pictures embedded in the file are supported.
- More in [AUTHORING.md](https://github.com/0xS4N4TG/kinetograph/blob/main/packages/cli/skills/kinetograph/references/AUTHORING.md), section 7.6.

## Can look different

- **`preserve-3d` with planes that cross.** Groups with `transform-style: preserve-3d` under `perspective` are drawn back to front, with no depth buffer. Planes that cross each other are drawn wrong.
- **Inline-block with a stacking context is drawn twice.** A `display: inline-block` element that also has a `transform`, or any other property that makes it a stacking context or positions it, comes out as two identical layers. Its antialiased edges look slightly heavier, and it costs two draws. To reproduce it: a div with the text "ab", and a `span` with `display: inline-block; transform: scale(0.5)`. The end card of the launch film (frames 1386 to 1520) has this case.
- **`@paper-design/shaders` is untested.** The engine has the methods this library needs, but the library itself has not been run through it. Its WebGL2 calls, such as `getShaderPrecisionFormat`, are untested.
- **A rare difference with `--concurrency 2`.** In one render out of several, `--concurrency 2` produced a different video from the same project. Other runs at 2 workers matched, and runs at 1, 3, 4 and 6 workers matched. The cause is not known. It may be a rare race, or a fault in that one run. Renders are meant to be identical at any concurrency, so a difference is a bug.
