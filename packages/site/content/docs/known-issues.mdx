Each item says what doesn't work and what you see instead. For what does work, see [Remotion compatibility](/docs/compat/). Items marked "reported" show up in the warnings and in `doctor`.

## Not planned

- **`IFrame`.** Not drawn, because a real webpage needs a browser. It is reported as unsupported content.
- **Not exported.** `Internals`, `FolderContext`, `PathInternals` and `AudioForPreview` are internal to Remotion.
- **Replaced by Kinetograph's own tools.** `@remotion/three` and `@remotion/player` are replaced by the engine's own 3D layer and studio. `renderer`, `bundler`, `cli` and `lambda` are replaced by the Kinetograph CLI.
- **Tailwind packages.** `tailwind` and `tailwind-v4` are webpack configuration only, so they do nothing here. Tailwind itself works.

## Partly working

### Effects and `HtmlInCanvas`

- `2d`, `webgl2` and `webgpu` effects (`createEffect`) run, and so do the `onPaint` and `onInit` props, with `getContext('webgpu')` in them. A WebGPU effect gets a recording `gpuDevice` (compute passes and storage textures included, as under WebGPU below); the picture before it is a source for `queue.copyExternalImageToTexture` (an upload from the layer, so the destination is `rgba8unorm` or `rgba8unorm-srgb`).
- A `webgpu` effect, `onInit` or `onPaint` that makes a call listed under WebGPU below as not drawn is drawn without the effect (or without `onPaint`) and reported.
- State an effect keeps from `setup` does not carry to the next frame. Each frame runs `setup`, `apply` and `cleanup` again.
- `getImageData` of the source is not supported.
- An `async` `apply`, `onInit` or `onPaint` holds the frame until its promise settles (as `delayRender` does), so what it draws after an `await` is in the frame, the same alone as in a sequence. `onPaint` waits for `onInit`, and the cleanup `onInit` returns runs after `onPaint`. A rejection is reported for that frame and the picture is drawn without the stage. The stage runs once per frame; promises that wait on a timer need real time, not `setTimeout`, which the render's virtual clock does not advance.
- An `<HtmlInCanvas>` inside another is not supported.

### Libraries

- Mermaid 12 draws flowcharts, class, state and entity-relationship diagrams with its default HTML labels (a `<foreignObject>` of `<div>`s) and sequence diagrams and pie charts (text labels); checked on those six. The label text is measured and drawn with the registered font, or the built-in fallback when its family (`"Recursive Variable"`, `arial`) is not loaded, so register the font you want before `mermaid.render` and the boxes fit it. Gaps: a `<foreignObject>` with a percentage `width` or `height` is not drawn (`doctor` says so), a `display: table` label is a block that holds its content in one cell (no row or column sizing), and `mermaid.initialize({ htmlLabels: false })` still gives plain SVG text if you want no HTML at all.
- `monospace` text falls back to a proportional face, so code blocks (Shiki, Code Hike) look wider than in Chrome unless the project loads a monospace font, for example with `@remotion/google-fonts/JetBrainsMono`.

### Video pixels

`onVideoFrame` (on `<Video>`, `<OffthreadVideo>` and `<Html5Video>`) gives your code the frame the video shows, decoded by the engine's own decoder. Draw it with `drawImage` and read it with `getImageData`.

- The frame is a function of the composition frame. It is called once for each frame the video is shown in, after the frame's effects have run, and a canvas you draw on starts each frame empty. The `VideoFrame` of WebCodecs is not what you get; it has `width`, `height`, `timestamp` and `close()` and nothing else.
- `getImageData` of a canvas that holds one plain `drawImage` of a frame or an image file is read on the CPU, resampled like the renderer's bilinear filter. It is the same bytes at 1:1 and within 1 level of the GPU's draw when the picture is scaled. A draw with a shadow, a filter, alpha, a composite operation, a rotation, a box off the pixel grid, a nearest-neighbour shrink, or anything drawn after it is drawn by the renderer and read back, as before, which costs a few milliseconds more.
- A frame is decoded as the video layer decodes it: a file with an alpha channel (ProRes 4444) keeps it, one without is opaque. A video whose colour space is not named is converted as a video layer is, which differs from Chrome's colour management.
- `texImage2D` and `copyExternalImageToTexture` take the frame; WebGL reads its pixels first, so a WebGL canvas that uploads a new frame every frame pays the decode and a copy.
- The frame of a `<video>` element you create is the one at its `currentTime`; the engine does not play it.
- `onVideoFrame` of `@remotion/webcodecs` `convertMedia` is a different API (a WebCodecs `VideoFrame`) and still throws.

### Video and audio encoding and decoding in your own code

`@remotion/webcodecs` is Remotion's wrapper around the browser's WebCodecs API, which encodes and decodes video and audio frames. Its encode and decode functions, and the box tree and sample callbacks of `@remotion/media-parser`, throw `UnsupportedApiError`. They need a video decoder and encoder in the addon, which is not built. Normal `<Video>` and `<Audio>` are not affected.

### Hardware encoding on Intel and AMD GPUs (Linux)

The ffmpeg built into the addon has no VAAPI or Quick Sync encoders, so `--encoder h264_vaapi` used to fail with `this ffmpeg has no encoder called "h264_vaapi"`. They now run in your system's ffmpeg, as `libx264` does:

```sh
KINETOGRAPH_FFMPEG=$(which ffmpeg) npx kinetograph render scene.json -o out.mp4 --encoder h264_vaapi
```

`h264_vaapi`, `hevc_vaapi`, `h264_qsv` and `hevc_qsv` are supported this way, for 4:2:0 pictures (`yuv420p`, and `yuv420p10le` for `hevc_*`). They are never the default, so a Linux machine with a GPU still renders with `libopenh264` unless you ask. Before an encoder is accepted, a four-picture test encode must open; if it does not, the error shows what ffmpeg said and the usual fix (the driver package, membership of the `render` group, `vainfo`). VAAPI uses the first `/dev/dri/renderD*`; set `KINETOGRAPH_VAAPI_DEVICE=/dev/dri/renderD129` to use another GPU.

- The output depends on the GPU and driver, unlike `libx264`'s, so the same project can encode to different bytes on two machines. `--crf N` is mapped to the encoder's constant quality (VAAPI `-qp`, Quick Sync `-global_quality`), which is the same scale as a CRF but not the same picture quality.
- Not in the addon's own ffmpeg because linking libva would stop the addon loading on any machine without `libva.so.2`. A way to do it without that risk (loading libva at run time) is written up in `docs/proposals/linux-hw-encode-linked.md` in the repository.
- Verified by unit tests of the command lines and a stand-in ffmpeg. The first run against real Intel and AMD hardware is still to come: if one fails on yours, the error text and `ffmpeg -encoders | grep -E 'vaapi|qsv'` are what to report.

### Skia on the CPU when the GPU is Vulkan (Linux, Windows)

Skia's Graphite draws on the GPU through Vulkan. When it cannot start on a device, drawing falls back to Skia's CPU backend, which draws the same scene more slowly and with slightly different anti-aliasing. The one-time warning, and `kinetograph doctor` (also `--json`, as `rendererReason`), now say why: the GPU and driver, and which Vulkan version, function or image-format usage is missing. For example `Skia's Graphite cannot use the Vulkan device Intel(R) Graphics (MTL), Mesa 24.2.1, Vulkan 1.3.274: R8G8B8A8_UNORM images lack the format features COLOR_ATTACHMENT_BLEND`. When every check passes and Graphite still refuses, the message says so rather than naming a cause. An Intel Arc (Meteor Lake) machine that falls back is the case this was written for; send the line it prints. `KINETOGRAPH_SKIA_CPU=1` chooses the CPU on purpose.

### HyperFrames projects

- **Three.js.** Not drawn. A page that loads Three.js is reported on every frame.
- **WebGPU.** Shader blocks that use `shaders.iife.js` (`aurora`, `blob`, `ripples`, `swirl` and the like) are drawn natively. See [WebGPU](#webgpu).
- **Import maps and ES modules.** Run (see the compat page). Not supported: CSS or JSON import attributes. An import map with a `src` is ignored, by design: Chrome does not load external import maps either (it never requests the file, and the imports the map would have named fail), so put the map's JSON inside the tag. A module graph must be in the cache. An `import()` of a specifier built at run time (`` import(`./locale/${lang}.js`) ``) is found by running the first frames of the page in the pre-pass (frame 0, the last and up to six between): one that shows only in a frame not sampled is downloaded when a render first asks for it, locked and noted, and with `--offline` it is an error that names the specifier. A module loaded this way is bundled on its own, so a library it shares with the page's other modules is a second copy.
- **Selectors that reach into an `<svg>` from outside it.** The engine applies the page's CSS cascade to an `<svg>`, including rules that match through an ancestor, shapes a script builds after load, and rules inside `@media`.
- **Timing.** `data-start` references stay inside one composition. A `rate` lane on audio is played in quarter-second slices at constant rates.
- **Remote pictures set by a script.** A picture a script sets (`img.src = url`, `srcset`, `poster`, an SVG `href`, `el.style.backgroundImage`) is read from the pre-download cache, like one in the markup. The pre-download step finds the URL when the page's own script writes it out (`'https://cdn.example/a.png'`), and runs the page's first frames (frame 0, the last and up to six between) when the script builds it (`base + name + '.png'`). A picture whose URL shows only on a frame that is not sampled, or whose host is not allowed, is drawn without and reported on every frame, and `doctor` gives the `fetch-deps` command that downloads it (with `--allow-host` for a host that is not a default one). A render never downloads it.
- **Colour grading.** Every control of `data-color-grading` is drawn by the same shader maths as HyperFrames' own (see the compat page). The `effects` list (blur, pixelate, chroma bleed, tape damage, film artifacts, halftone, two-ink print, ascii, dither, bloom, mono screen, scanlines, chromatic aberration, CRT curvature, digital glitch, engraving, crosshatch, kuwahara) is drawn too, with all 64 controls and the palette. One difference from HyperFrames: the radii of blur, bloom and kuwahara count canvas pixels of the layer, so on a layer scaled by a transform they scale with it. A `.cube` file that is missing or unreadable drops the `lut` and says so. The grain's noise comes from the same hash as HyperFrames' shader, so it matches on a GPU whose `sin` agrees with Chrome's (Metal does).
- **Variable-font advance widths.** Text in a variable font that sets `font-variation-settings` per element measures about 13% wider than in Chrome for `Recursive` (`weight-wave`), so a line fitted by `measureText` wraps.
- **Fallback fonts cover symbols, not scripts.** A character the font lacks falls back to Noto Sans Symbols 2, Noto Sans Math and Noto Emoji, which ship with Kinetograph. CJK, Arabic, Devanagari and the like have no bundled font: such text is drawn as boxes and reported (`font-family`, "no glyph for U+4E2D ..."), so load a font that has it. Emoji are monochrome. Text in the optional Skia addon (`<Paragraph>`, `<Text>`) uses only the fonts you register, with no fallback. SVG `<text>` falls back too but does not report a character no font has.
- **Slow load.** A page that reads thousands of computed styles used to restyle the whole island for every element asked about. The addon now keeps the island of the last readback and edits it when the tree changes, so `claude-exchange` takes about 1.7 s before frame 0 (it took 12.5 s before; 20 s on a debug build). A page that rewrites many styles between reads still restyles what changed each time.

### Canvas 2D

- A `url()` filter is drawn when it names an SVG `<filter>` of the page. On a `<Canvas>` component, which has no page, or when nothing has that id, it is reported and the rest of the filter list is drawn.
- `isPointInStroke` agrees with Chrome's on straight edges. Along a curve, a point within a tenth of a pixel of the edge can get the other answer, because Chrome approximates the curve's outline.

### WebGL

Every frame starts with an empty canvas and blank framebuffer textures, also for a context made with `preserveDrawingBuffer`, because a frame is a function of its own number. A frame that draws nothing draws nothing, where a browser would keep the last picture. Each item below needs new GPU work.

- Not supported: multisampled renderbuffers (`renderbufferStorageMultisample` with samples above 0); targets that are not RGBA or that hold uploaded pixels; attachments of different sizes in one framebuffer; `generateMipmap` on a texture drawn into; a `TEXTURE_BASE_LEVEL` other than 0; `LINES`, `LINE_STRIP` and `LINE_LOOP`; instanced points; a `vertexAttribDivisor` above 1; cube, 3D and float textures; `sampler2DShadow`; and multisampled edges.
- `readPixels` is refused by design.
- The depth and stencil tests are drawn, on the canvas (its `depth` and `stencil` attributes) and on framebuffers, with every function and operation. The depth buffer holds 32-bit floats where a browser's holds 24 bits, so two surfaces closer than about 6e-8 of the depth range can order differently, and `polygonOffset`'s `units` are whole steps of that float buffer, truncated to an integer. A canvas made with `depth: false` has no depth buffer, so the test always passes.
- A depth texture is sampled `NEAREST`, whatever filter it is given.
- Points are drawn as squares of `gl_PointSize` pixels with `gl_PointCoord`, at most 511 pixels across (`ALIASED_POINT_SIZE_RANGE` is 1 to 511, as Chrome reports on Apple silicon). `gl_VertexID` in a vertex shader that draws points is the point's index, but with `drawElements` it is the position in the index list, not the index.
- `texSubImage2D` over a texture that had `generateMipmap` makes its mipmaps again from the new picture; Chrome keeps the old levels until the next `generateMipmap`.

### WebGPU

A canvas's `getContext('webgpu')` records, and the passes it submits are drawn on the engine's own device. Drawn: buffers (uniform, storage, vertex, index), WGSL modules, bind group layouts, render and compute pipelines (blend, topology, culling, vertex buffers, `layout: 'auto'`), bind groups, render passes into the canvas or into a 2D texture, compute passes, `draw`, `drawIndexed`, `dispatchWorkgroups`, viewport, scissor, `writeBuffer`, `copyBufferToBuffer`, `clearBuffer`, and textures and samplers:

- 2D textures of the 8, 16 and 32-bit colour formats (`rgba8unorm`, `rgba8unorm-srgb`, `bgra8unorm`, `bgra8unorm-srgb`, `r8unorm`, `rg8unorm`, `rgba16float`, `rgba32float`, `r32float`, `rg32float`, the `uint` and `sint` ones, and the rest of WebGPU's table without optional features), with mip levels; `createView` (levels), `createSampler` (address modes, filters, lod clamps, anisotropy); texture and sampler bind group entries, in explicit layouts and in `layout: 'auto'`.
- `queue.writeTexture`, `copyBufferToTexture`, `copyTextureToTexture`, and `queue.copyExternalImageToTexture` from `ImageData`, `<img>`, `ImageBitmap` (`createImageBitmap` of an image, a canvas, `ImageData` or a `Blob` of an image file), a 2D `<canvas>` or `OffscreenCanvas`, a `<video>` and a layer (in an effect). A video gives the frame at `video.currentTime` (set it before the copy; the frame is the one a video layer shows at that time, so it is the same alone and in a sequence). `flipY` and `premultipliedAlpha` are honoured, and `copyExternalImageToTexture` goes into the four 8-bit RGBA formats only.
- Render passes into a texture, so multi-pass shaders work. A texture is the steps that wrote it, in order; the steps of an earlier frame stay for later frames until a whole write (a `clear` pass, a whole `writeTexture` or copy) covers them, so a page that redraws a texture each frame is the same alone and in a sequence. A texture drawn with `loadOp: 'load'` every frame piles up and is not: the frame then depends on the frames before it.
- Every size, level, origin, row stride and usage is checked as the spec does, on the page side (it throws) and again when the frame is drawn (a warning on stderr and a transparent canvas). A frame may hold 4 GiB of textures (all levels counted: three of the biggest textures the device's 8192 limit allows, `rgba32float` with every level) and 4096 steps; identical bytes are kept once. WebGPU has no such cap and Chrome made 4 GiB of textures on the M4 without an error; the engine keeps one because each render worker makes a frame's textures anew.

- Compute: `createComputePipeline`, `createComputePipelineAsync` (with an explicit layout or `layout: 'auto'` and `getBindGroupLayout`), `beginComputePass`, `setPipeline`, `setBindGroup` (dynamic offsets too), `dispatchWorkgroups` and `end`, replayed in submit order with the render passes and copies. Storage buffers (`read-only-storage` and `storage`) and storage textures (`write-only`; `read-write` and `read-only` for `r32float`, `r32uint` and `r32sint`, as WebGPU allows without optional features) in `rgba32float`, `rgba16float`, `rgba8unorm`, `r32float` and the other storage formats. A 32-bit float texture is sampled as `unfilterable-float` with a non-filtering sampler, or as `float` when the device has `float32-filterable` (it does on Apple GPUs, and `adapter.features` says so).
- `device.limits` and `adapter.limits` are those of the engine's own wgpu device, asked of the addon; a device that asks for better limits is refused, because the one that draws has them. Workgroup sizes written with number literals, dispatch sizes, storage binding counts and sizes, offsets and formats are checked on the page side (the call throws) and again by `webgpu::validate` when the frame is drawn (a warning and a transparent canvas, never a panic). A frame holds at most 4096 dispatches and 2^26 workgroups.
- A buffer a pass writes (a `storage` binding that the layout or the WGSL says is written) is, like a texture, the steps that wrote it, so the frame does not depend on which frames were drawn. A frame holds only the steps its picture reads: those that wrote something nothing in it reads are left out, and a step that writes a whole texture level or buffer (a `clear` pass, a whole `writeTexture`, `writeBuffer` or copy) drops what it covers. A compute pass writes some unknown part of what it binds, so it does not cover: to be the same alone as in a sequence, a page clears or rewrites what its compute passes write (a `clear` render pass, `writeBuffer`, or a texture it makes anew) before it uses them in a frame.
- **State a page carries from frame to frame is replayed from its history and reported.** A page whose compute pass, or render pass into a texture, builds on what an earlier frame left (a simulation texture it updates each frame, `loadOp: 'load'`) is drawn right in a sequence, because the steps that made the state are replayed in order. A frame drawn alone, or by another worker, cannot have them: the page did not run the earlier frames. Each frame that reads such state says so (`doctor` lists it as WebGPU state a page carries). What the first frame of the run makes is taken as the page's setup and is not reported. A pass that binds a read-write storage buffer it never reads is kept as a dependency of every later frame (the engine cannot tell), and so is reported; bind only what the pass uses.
- Shaders with `atomic` operations whose result depends on the order the invocations run in are not deterministic on a GPU, and the engine does not make them so. `water`, `frost`, `nebula` and `goo`, measured on the M4, drew the same frames at 1, 2 and 4 workers and alone.

Not supported, and reported on every frame (`--strict` fails): query sets and timestamp writes, render bundles, indirect draws and dispatches, depth and stencil, multisampling, texture arrays, 1D and 3D textures, `viewFormats`, comparison samplers, `importExternalTexture` (copy the frame with `copyExternalImageToTexture`), a video that is not a file of the project, reading a buffer or a texture back (`mapAsync`, `copyTextureToBuffer`), a copy of a buffer a pass wrote into a texture (`copyBufferToTexture`), the canvas's own texture as a source or in a bind group, pipeline-overridable constants, and other texture formats (depth, compressed, and the ones that need optional features, such as `bgra8unorm` and `r16unorm` storage).

- A `<video>` is read at `currentTime`, which a page sets itself; the engine does not play it. A video file that names no colour space is converted as a video layer is, which differs from Chrome's colour management for unlabelled video.
- An `rgba16float` canvas is drawn at half-float precision all the way into the compositor, and a `display-p3` `colorSpace` is converted to the video's sRGB primaries. The output is SDR sRGB (Rec. 709) video, so values outside 0 to 1 are clipped, as a canvas of the standard tone mapping is, and a P3 colour outside sRGB's gamut is clipped to it. `toneMapping: { mode: 'extended' }` is drawn as standard and reported.
- A WGSL module naga refuses is reported and the canvas draws nothing; one that wgpu refuses later is a warning on stderr and a transparent canvas.
- The `shaders.iife.js` blocks of HyperFrames draw into one canvas without an input: none of them takes an image or a layer. The ones that read a texture they made themselves (`chrome-orb`, `frost`, `goo`, `heatmap`, `hologram`, `holographic`, `light-edge`, `liquid-metal`, `nebula`, `obsidian`, `plastic`, `thin-film`, `water`) also dispatch compute kernels that write `rgba32float` storage textures; they are drawn, with nothing reported. They ask the adapter for `timestamp-query`, which is not offered (query sets are not drawn), and say so on stderr.

### WGSL `shader()`

It sees only its own layer, runs one pass, has no other texture, and takes `f32` uniforms only.

### 3D (`scene3d`)

- A model's texture on a second set of texture coordinates, `alphaMode: MASK` (drawn opaque) and glTF extensions are not read, and are reported.
- Every light may cast shadows (up to 48 maps; a point light takes 6), but a scene's shadow maps may hold at most 1 GiB, every map counted at the largest `mapSize`: 48 maps of 2048 or 16 of 4096. A scene over it is refused when it loads.
- A bevel bigger than a concave curve of the outline folds over itself there, as in three.js.

### CSS

- A `box-shadow` blur above about 200 px is a true Gaussian here; Chrome approximates very wide box shadows and draws them fainter. `filter: blur()`, `drop-shadow()` and `backdrop-filter: blur()` match Chrome up to its own limit of 532 px, and past it, as Chrome draws them 532 px wide.
- A margin makes room for at most `3 * 128` px past the canvas edge for a scene `glow`, shadow or canvas-mode `backdropBlur`: a wider one is drawn as wide, but content further off the canvas than that does not reach in. CSS `backdrop-filter` reads only its own box, so it needs no margin.
- A `backdrop-filter` in a `preserve-3d` context that sits inside an element drawn as one group (an ancestor with `opacity` below 1, a `filter`, a `mask`, `mix-blend-mode` or `isolation: isolate`) blurs only what its own 3D context drew before it, not the rest of that group. Chrome blurs everything the group drew before it. The frame reports it as approximated.

### SVG

- A page rule reaches the shapes in an `<svg>` for fill, stroke, opacity, display, visibility, the font, `text-anchor`, `paint-order`, `shape-rendering`, `clip-rule`, `transform` (with `rotate`, `scale`, `translate` and `transform-origin`), `filter: url(#id)`, `clip-path: url(#id)` and `mask: url(#id)`. A CSS filter function (`blur()`) or a basic shape (`inset()`) on an element inside the `<svg>` is not drawn.
- A rule that sets a property to its initial value on a shape with a presentation attribute for it (`.bar { fill: black }` over `fill="red"`) does not win: the value looks like inheritance.

### GSAP

- GSAP's root clock follows the frame, so a `gsap.to` that plays by itself advances ([GSAP](/docs/compat/#gsap)). A jump to a later frame (a still, a worker starting at frame 600) renders the frames it skips, without drawing or reading them, so that an effect which makes a tween at an intermediate frame runs there. This can't be skipped without changing the frame: an effect that starts a tween at frame 30 has to run at frame 30. It costs one React render per skipped frame in a project that imports `gsap`, also when it only seeks paused timelines. Measured: about 0.1 ms per skipped frame for a 48-element composition (a still at frame 899 takes 100 ms more than one at frame 0), and 0.36 ms for the HyperFrames test page.
- Motion-blur sub-frames show a tween that plays by itself at the sub-frame's time, and run its `onUpdate`. They do not run `onStart`, `onComplete`, `onRepeat` or a timeline's `call()`: what those do would outlive the sample. A tween that starts later than the frame is not sampled in the frame's later sub-frames.
- `ScrollTrigger` and `Draggable` respond to scrolling and dragging, which a render does not have, so they do not apply. `Flip` works.
- More in [GSAP](/docs/compat/#gsap).

### Libraries

- A rendered frame is only as frame-pure as the library: Motion's `animate()` that plays by itself is (see [Libraries](/docs/compat/#libraries-that-run-their-own-frame-loop)), but one that reads the wall clock or a real `Date` outside the virtual one is not.
- The Web Animations API (`element.animate()`, which Motion uses) does not draw an animation of a pseudo-element (`pseudoElement: '::before'`); it warns once. An animation that a later one covers completely is not removed by itself (`replaceState` stays `active`), so `getAnimations()` keeps listing it until it is cancelled. Two transform lists whose functions don't pair up mix through their 2D matrices; a 3D function there is not mixed and jumps at half way.
- Motion components that mount in the middle of a video (in a `<Sequence>`) start their animation at the frame they mount on. A still or a render worker that starts after that frame mounts them at its own first frame, so the animation starts there. Mount them at frame 0, or drive them from the frame with `@kinetographdev/motion`.
- Chart.js, Recharts and the rest measure text only with the fonts you registered. With none, text is measured and drawn in the built-in font.

### `@remotion/skia` and `@shopify/react-native-skia`

- Text inside an SVG is drawn with your registered fonts. A family that is not registered is drawn in the built-in fallback font and reported. Characters the font lacks are not drawn.
- Reanimated values live for one frame, so drive them from `useCurrentFrame()`. `withTiming`, `withSpring` and the other clock animations jump to their end value and are reported.
- `useVideo` shows the video's frames but does not play its sound. Add an `<Audio>` of the file for it.
- Not supported: `makeImageFromView`, `Skia.SVG.MakeFromData`, and touch handling.
- Drawing is on the CPU only. Skia's GPU path was measured and kept out: no prebuilt Skia has the GPU backend and the text and SVG modules together, and GPU pixels differ by machine.
- An offscreen surface is drawn on the CPU too, so it looks the same on every machine.
- PNGs of drawn frames are kept under `~/.cache/kinetograph/skia`, the newest 400 to 600 of them.
- More in [AUTHORING.md](https://github.com/0xS4N4TG/kinetograph/blob/main/packages/cli/skills/kinetograph/references/AUTHORING.md), section 7.7.

### `@remotion/rive`

- Set state machine inputs with the `stateMachineInputs` prop, as an object or as a function of the frame.
- A referenced picture or font is read from beside the `.riv` file or given by `assetLoader`. An asset on Rive's CDN is not fetched; it is reported and drawn without.
- `getRenderer()` on the `ref` returns `null`.
- More in [AUTHORING.md](https://github.com/0xS4N4TG/kinetograph/blob/main/packages/cli/skills/kinetograph/references/AUTHORING.md), section 7.6.

## Can look different

- **`preserve-3d` planes cut along a box.** Planes in a `preserve-3d` context are ordered and cut where they cross, as Chrome's BSP tree does, using each element's border box. A shadow or glow that reaches past the box does not count as part of the plane when deciding what crosses. The line where two planes cross is not anti-aliased: each pixel along it belongs to one piece, as with Chrome's split quads. A layer whose pieces are cut more than 16 times (a card lying flat under a comb of 20 standing cards) is cut before it is warped instead, so its pieces meet with bilinear edges and can show a faint seam.
- **`backdrop-filter` on a turned element.** The backdrop is mirrored at the element's bounding box on the canvas, not at its own turned or skewed edges as in Chrome.
- **`@paper-design/shaders`: eight shaders checked.** Version 0.0.82 runs, and its mesh gradient, grain gradient, warp, voronoi, swirl, simplex noise, dot orbit and smoke ring (three of them read the library's noise texture) match Chrome for Testing to the pixel, mounted with `speed` 0 at a fixed `frame`. Its other shaders, the ones that take a picture, and `@paper-design/shaders-react` have not been run.
- **`ex` from a font that is not loaded.** The `ex` unit is the x-height of the element's first font. When the page names only a generic family (`serif`), that font is Kinetograph's built-in fallback, whose x-height (0.495 em) is larger than Chrome's Times (0.448 em), so a MathJax formula, which MathJax sizes in `ex`, comes out about 10% larger, and nothing reports it. Load the font the page asks for: with the same font loaded, MathJax's SVG output matches Chrome. Kinetograph reads the x-height from the font's OS/2 table, as Chrome does on Linux and Windows; Chrome on macOS measures the `x` glyph instead, which differs by a few percent in some fonts (KaTeX_Main: 0.442 em against 0.431 em).
- **`ex` inside SVG geometry.** A length in `ex` in an SVG shape's attributes (`<rect width="10ex">`) is half an em, not the font's x-height. The `width` and `height` of the `<svg>` element itself are right.
- **A rare one-level difference in a few pixels, when renderers share the GPU.** Two full renders of the same project at the same concurrency can differ in a few pixels of one frame, by 1 in one colour channel (the launch film's wall of chips in shot 8, frames 747 to 775, has shown it: 2 frames in about 35 full renders, and in a stress run that draws such frames over and over with 3 renderers in one process, about 1 draw in 1,000). It was first seen with `--concurrency 2` and `--concurrency 4`. It is not a result of what a worker drew before: a fresh renderer shows it on its second frame, drawing the frame again straight after gives the right pixels, and it shows with the layer cache and frame reuse off. It was not seen with one renderer in 12,000 draws. With `KINETOGRAPH_SKIA_CPU=1` (Skia's CPU backend) it was not seen in 58,000 draws, so it comes from drawing with Skia on the GPU (Graphite on Metal) while another renderer uses the GPU. Making Skia wait for the GPU before and after its work does not remove it, and neither do Metal's validation layers. The cause is not found. Until it is, a render that must be bit-exact can use `--concurrency 1`, or `KINETOGRAPH_SKIA_CPU=1` (slower on most scenes, and its pixels differ slightly from the GPU's).
