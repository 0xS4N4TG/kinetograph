---
title: Voice, music and sound
description: How to ask for narration, music and sound effects, tie picture to the voice, and check the mix.
---

Kinetograph mixes the audio files you give it and the sounds your code synthesises. It doesn't make speech or timings for you, so bring a voice file, a track and, for captions, an SRT or VTT file, and tell the agent where each one is.

## Bring the voice

Record it, or make it with a speech tool you already use, and put the file in `public/audio/`. Say what it is and what it says:

```txt title="Suggested prompt"
/kinetograph-markup The narration is public/audio/vo.wav and the script is in script.md. Start it at 0:01. Show the line "Ship it on Friday" on screen when it is spoken, and tell me the frame it starts on.
```

If the agent has a speech tool of its own, name it in the prompt and say which voice. Don't assume Kinetograph has one.

## Pace the picture to the voice

Give the agent the time each line starts, from the script or from a transcript. "The third card appears on the word 'Search', at 0:05.4" is something it can place exactly. Ask it to tie each reveal to a frame and show a strip across the lines.

For captions, the timings come from your speech-to-text tool as an SRT or VTT file. See [Text, captions and code](/docs/tips/text-captions-code/).

## Music

Say the mood, give a track by path, and say how loud it sits under the voice:

```txt title="Suggested prompt"
/kinetograph-markup Put public/audio/bed.mp3 under the whole video at 30% volume. Fade it in over the first second and out over the last two. Lower it to 12% while the narration plays, then run audio analyze.
```

`Audio` takes a `volume` that can change with the frame, so a fade or a dip under speech is a few lines, and it lasts exactly as long as you say. For a track with a beat, give the beat times in seconds and ask for cuts on them. The Studio script panel takes a track with its beats for a music-led piece.

## Sound effects

Tie each effect to a moment, not to the video in general: "a click on each card landing, a whoosh on the slide at 0:08". Too many effects blur together, so ask for them on the three or four moments that matter.

You have two sources:

- **Your own files.** Put them in `public/audio/` and name them.
- **Synthesised.** Kinetograph runs Web Audio code at a frame with `onFrame`, so the agent can make a click, a whoosh or a hit from oscillators and filtered noise. The noise is seeded, so it is the same every render. This runs on the CPU and needs no files.

## Video with its own sound

`Video` plays the sound of its file, with `volume`, `muted` and `toneFrequency`. For picture-in-picture, say where and how big: "the clip in the bottom right corner, 400 px wide, 40 px from each edge, muted".

## Say what "no sound" means

"No narration" and "silent" are different. "No narration, music only" and "no audio at all" both leave no doubt. A render where everything is muted and no `onFrame` runs has no audio stream.

## Check the mix

Describe what you hear, not the fix: "the music drowns the voice at 0:12". Then ask for the numbers:

```sh
npx kinetograph audio analyze src/index.tsx --scenes 0..90,90..180
```

It reports integrated loudness (LUFS), true peak, clipped samples and silences, for the whole mix and for each scene you list. Ask the agent to run it after a change to the music level, and to fix any clipped samples first. See [Audio](/docs/making-videos/audio/).

## What Kinetograph doesn't do

- It doesn't generate speech or transcribe. Bring the files.
- It doesn't clean noise or de-ess a recording. Fix the file first.
- `@remotion/webcodecs` encode and decode and `@remotion/media-parser` sample callbacks aren't supported. See [What works](/docs/compat/#video-and-audio).
