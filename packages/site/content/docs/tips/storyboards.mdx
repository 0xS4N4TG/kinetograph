---
title: Storyboards
description: How to plan a multi-scene video once, so every scene follows the same direction, and how to use Studio's storyboard panel for it.
---

For a video with more than a few scenes, state the film's shape once, give one direction block that every scene inherits, and give each scene a job. Settle that plan before any animation, because it is cheaper to change a line of text than a render.

## The film's shape

Say these four things first:

- **The point.** What a viewer should remember, in one sentence.
- **The arc.** How it moves: a problem, then a turn, then a payoff.
- **The audience.** Who watches, and where (a vertical feed, a keynote screen).
- **The mood.** One or two words, and the length.

## One direction block

Write the rules that every scene follows, once, and tell the agent to apply them to all of them:

- **Palette.** The colours and where each is used: "cream background, one accent, used only for the thing that matters".
- **Type.** The families and the sizes for headline and label.
- **Reveal timing.** How long things take to enter, and how long they hold.
- **One breather.** A single scene with nothing moving, near the middle or before the turn. It lets the rest land.
- **Banned.** What you don't want anywhere: "no stock gradients, no drop shadows on text, no spinning logos".

## A job for each scene

For each scene, one line each:

- **What it is.** A title, a proof, a demo, a list, an end card.
- **What it should make the viewer feel or understand.**
- **The focal point.** The one thing the eye should land on.
- **The elements.** What is on screen, and which is the hero and which is support.
- **Seconds.** How long it runs, and how it joins the next scene.

## Plant something, then call it back

Pick a small thing in the first scene, such as a line, a shape or a sound, and bring it back in the last. The video feels planned, and you spent one line on it: "the thin accent line that draws in scene 1 draws out again under the end card".

## An example

```txt title="Suggested prompt"
/kinetograph-markup Plan a 24 second vertical video, 1080 by 1920, for a budgeting app. Don't animate yet. Write the plan to plan/storyboard.md for me to approve.

Film: the point is that you always know what is left to spend. Arc: confusion, then one number, then calm. Audience: people on a short-video feed, sound off. Mood: calm, confident.

Direction for every scene: background #f6f0e4, text #1d1a16, accent #e0553a used only for the number that matters. Newsreader for the headline at 120 px, Inter for labels at 44 px. Entrances take 14 frames with an ease-out. Nothing bounces, nothing has a shadow. Scene 4 is the breather: nothing moves for 2 seconds.

Scenes:
1. 0:00 to 0:05, a hook. A pile of receipts and the words "Where did it go?". Focal point: the words. Draws a thin accent line under them.
2. 0:05 to 0:12, the turn. The pile collapses into one number, "412 left". Focal point: the number, in the accent.
3. 0:12 to 0:18, the proof. Three rows, "Rent", "Food", "Fun", each filling to a bar over 20 frames, one after the other.
4. 0:18 to 0:21, the breather. The number alone.
5. 0:21 to 0:24, the end. The logo from public/images/logo.svg and "Know what's left". The accent line from scene 1 draws out under it.
```

When you approve it, say "build it, hook and turn first, then show me a strip of each".

## Plan in Studio

Studio has panels for this. Ask the agent to open them, and it waits for your answer in the window:

- A **brief** panel for purpose, audience, platform, size, length and the one takeaway.
- A **script** panel for the lines and their timing.
- A **storyboard** panel with a card for each shot: what it shows, how it moves, the line spoken, its seconds and the transition into the next. You can edit, remove and approve.
- A **styleframes** panel that shows two or three looks as frames, so you pick one before any scene is built.
- A **review** panel that sits beside the preview, where you leave notes on frames of a composition and approve it or ask for changes.

```txt title="Suggested prompt"
/kinetograph-studio Open Studio on src/index.tsx. Start with a brief for a 24 second launch video, then a storyboard, and wait for my answers in the panels before you animate anything.
```

Your answers are saved in `plan/` next to the project, so a restart doesn't lose them. See [Panels](/docs/studio/panels/).

## Common mistakes

- Describing each scene on its own, so the look drifts.
- No breather, so the whole video runs at one level.
- Animating before the plan is approved.
- A scene with three focal points.
