A panel is a box in [Studio](/docs/studio/). An **agent panel** is a question your coding agent puts to you: a brief, a script, a storyboard, a choice of looks or a review. A **plugin panel** is an editor a plugin draws for one field of a composition's props.

## Agent panels

Ask for one in your prompt. The agent opens it, waits, and gets your answer as its next message. The answers are saved under `plan/` beside the project's entry file.

```txt title="Suggested prompt"
/kinetograph-studio Open a brief panel for a 30 second launch video and wait for my answers before you plan anything.
```

| Panel | What you decide |
|---|---|
| `brief` | Purpose, audience, platform, size, length, the one takeaway, and references |
| `script` | The lines of the script and their seconds, or a music track with its beats |
| `storyboard` | A card for each shot: what it shows, how it moves, its line, seconds and transition |
| `styleframes` | Which of several looks to use, or how to mix them |
| `review` | Notes on frames of a composition, then approve or ask for changes |
| `custom` | Whatever the agent lays out, such as a rating for each shot |

A stage strip shows where the video stands: Brief, Script, Board, Style, Animatic, Build, Deliver. You can put a panel away and bring it back from the strip. If the agent is still working when you answer, your answer waits in the **Up next** tray.

For a new video, ask the agent to go in order: brief, script, storyboard, styleframes, then a review of a rough cut before it animates. Skip a stage that doesn't fit. A caption fix needs no brief. [Storyboards](/docs/tips/storyboards/) shows how to write the plan that goes into these panels.

### A custom panel

To ask for one yourself, say so: "open a panel where I rate each shot from 1 to 5". The agent builds it from a choice, a slider, a text box, a list, a table or notes drawn on a frame. It is data, so nothing it sends runs.

## Plugin panels

A [plugin](/docs/plugins/) can draw its own editor for a field. Studio shows the field as a card in the **Props** tab, with the plugin's panel inside it, in place of the generated form. The panel can also draw guides and handles on the preview. If one throws, Studio shows an error box and the rest of the form keeps working.

To write one, see [Writing a plugin](/docs/plugins/writing-a-plugin/#the-panel).
